## 1. THE REAL PROBLEM (verified only)

Jacob's thesis is **mostly true, and true where it matters most.** An AWS partner's entire economy (co-sell engagement, tier, discounts, MDF, and the Partner Opportunity Acceleration Funds) is gated on the single act of **originating and registering net-new customer opportunities in ACE**, and AWS enriches and scores the opportunities a partner brings it but never originates the customer list. That is not Forj framing, it is on AWS's own pages: the ACE program page ties MDF and the acceleration funds to "originating new customer opportunities," the Services tiers require a count of **launched** opportunities (3 at Select, 20 at Advanced, 50 at Premier, over a rolling 12 months), and AWS's June 2026 lead-enrichment/prospecting feature scores leads already sitting in Partner Central rather than handing over a net-new target list. AWS itself said in December 2024 that startup partners "need help getting their products to market, both by validating their technology and generating net new business." So **yes**: net-new opportunity **sourcing** is a real, AWS-acknowledged, documented constraint, and **yes**: manufacturing ACE-ready opportunities is the load-bearing job. The one honest narrowing: the binding constraint is **top-of-funnel sourcing**, not co-sell or delivery, and the tier gate counts launched opportunities of **any origin** (AWS never says "partner-originated") — partners simply have almost no AWS-originated deals, so in practice they must originate their own.

**Per segment:**
- **Startup / early-stage partner — YES, AWS-documented.** AWS's own words (Dec 2024) name this segment's pain directly. This is the strongest-evidenced segment.
- **Scale-up partner — PLAUSIBLE, not separately documented.** No AWS or named-analyst source singles out scale-ups. The pain is a reasonable inference from the same mechanics, but do not present it as evidenced. This is the soft spot in the three-segment cut.
- **AWS practice inside a larger SI / house — YES on the structure, via Canalys.** Canalys's "who should lead" segmentation puts AWS (not the SI) in front of greenfield and new-to-cloud buyers, while service partners lead already-identified transformation. The SI has delivery muscle on known accounts and is structurally weak at AWS-specific greenfield **sourcing**. This is Forj framing built on a verified Canalys table, so state it as reading, not as an AWS statement.

**One caveat that must ride along:** "AWS does no discovery" is **false** (Lead Prospecting shipped 2026). The only defensible line is "**AWS scores the list you bring it, it never hands you the list.**"

## 2. THE FUNDING LEVER (verified vs plausible)

The lever is **real and defensible on its mechanism, but not on any magnitude.** Build the pitch on the mechanism, never on a number.

**VERIFIED (primary source, safe for a public page or pitch):**
- Customer + partner funding attaches to a **named, registered opportunity**: MDF and Partner Opportunity Acceleration Funds are tied to "originating new customer opportunities" (ACE page). No account, no funded door. This proves the discovery problem sits **upstream** of the funding problem.
- **Each program is precondition-gated**, and the preconditions are close to mutually exclusive: MAP needs an existing on-prem or rival-cloud estate to migrate; PoC needs a customer not yet committed to AWS; the **Partner Greenfield Program (PGP)** needs a net-new / not-yet-on-AWS account; Activate needs a young startup. So "which funded door, if any" is a per-account **classification** problem. The mechanics are verified; "matching is the scarce skill" is Forj framing on a verified base.
- **MDF is a genuine ~50% reimbursement for third-party demand-gen**, not cash for a seat: up to 50% co-funding for **completed** eligible activities (email campaigns and telemarketing are explicitly eligible), the partner fronts the cost and is reimbursed after completion, the claim window is tight (within 30 days of completion or by Dec 15, cannot cross the calendar year, one self-extension up to 90 days), and the fund-request button is grayed out unless the partner has the right Partner Path status and a provisioned MDF wallet.
- **AWS keeps re-engineering the funding workflow to remove friction** (the July 2024 MAP template: fewer approval stages, simple workflow to $10M ARR, auto-created claim milestones), which is AWS's own revealed admission that it was slow and multi-stage.
- **Partners themselves rate the money as material:** Canalys (Nov 2023, n=303) found 55% of partners view MDF and migration funds as an important factor in closing new customer deals.
- **The target segment is the one leaving it on the table structurally:** The Channel Company "State of Partner Marketing 2025" found fewer than one-third of smaller partners have a dedicated marketing resource (52% run on part-time or shared staff), and an "MDF-admin-as-a-service" category exists on AWS's own Marketplace.

**PLAUSIBLE / NOT VERIFIED (never state as fact):**
- **The magnitude of underuse.** There is no primary source for "~60% of MDF goes unspent," "$35B unclaimed," or any "$X left on the table." These are channel-vendor lore laundered through blogs. Say "widely underused," never a figure.
- **PoC / GenAI PoC caps** ("~10% of year-one spend / $25K"): partner-blog figures, not on AWS's public funding page. Never quote a cap.
- **The internal-cost / retainer clause** that Forj's desk pricing leans on: "internal costs are ineligible, third-party receipts required" is corroborated but only from a 2019 **Amazon Confidential** Marketplace-Seller PDF, not a clean current public services-scoped citation; "retainers not eligible" is a Forj **inference** ("retainers" appears in no AWS source). Capture both verbatim from the login-gated Partner Central MDF Guide before either goes into a claim or a pitch.

**Bottom line:** the funding lever is a sound pitch spine (money is real, it is precondition-gated per account, it is hard to claim, small partners are too under-resourced to run it) but only as a **mechanism**, stripped of every magnitude number.

## 3. THE POSITIONING THAT FLOWS

**One-sentence promise (headline):**

> **Forj turns your market into named, ACE-ready AWS opportunities, and tells you which funded door (customer funding or MDF) each one opens, so you originate the net-new pipeline AWS scores but never hands you.**

The problem-to-solution line: AWS scores the list you bring it and never hands you the list; horizontal tools (ZoomInfo, Apollo, Clay) give you contacts but have no concept of ACE, co-sell eligibility, or which funding door an account opens; AWS-partner GTM vendors (Tackle, Labra, Clazar, WorkSpan) sit **downstream** of discovery and help you transact a deal you already have. Forj is the only layer that **manufactures the net-new account and names its ACE-ready packaging and its funded next step.** (Both competitor lines are Forj framing by product category, defensible as reasoning, not citable to one URL, so keep them as argument not as sourced fact.)

**Where "European reach / the lawful channel" sits:** it is the **HOW**, the proof and the moat underneath the promise, **not the headline.** The headline is the outcome (ACE-ready opportunities plus the activated funding). "We build the net-new list from national registries across Europe, and we do it lawfully, consent-aware and call-first where cold email needs consent (NO, DK)" is the mechanism that makes the outcome credible and hard to copy. Lead with the outcome; earn belief with the European reach and the lawful channel one layer down.
